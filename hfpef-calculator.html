Why HFpEF is harder to diagnose with obesity

Reference: van Dalen BM, Chin JF, Motiram PA, et al. Challenges in the diagnosis of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction in individuals with obesity. Cardiovasc Diabetol. 2025;24(1):71.

  • Natriuretic peptide levels are often falsely lowered by adiposity, so standard NT-proBNP/BNP cut-offs used in HFA-PEFF and H2FPEF may under-detect HFpEF.
  • BMI itself is a component of both H2FPEF and HFpEF-ABA, which can inflate probability scores independent of cardiac status in patients with obesity alone.
  • Echocardiographic image quality and chamber geometry assessment (LAVI, LV mass index, RWT) are frequently affected by body habitus.
  • Symptom overlap (dyspnoea, exercise intolerance, oedema) between obesity and HFpEF complicates the pre-test clinical gateway.
  • The authors propose applying the same major/minor, domain-based logic as HFA-PEFF (Functional, Morphological, Biomarker — each capped at 2 points) but with obesity-adjusted thresholds still under development.

Until a validated obesity-adjusted threshold set is published, consider running this patient through the H2FPEF and HFA-PEFF tabs while giving extra clinical weight to structural/functional echo findings over natriuretic peptide levels alone.
